PartialFSHT.jl

Partial-sky spherical harmonic transforms: the operator

\[(\mathcal{Y}_R a)_i = \sum_{\ell m} a_{\ell m} Y_{\ell m}(\Omega_i)\]

on an arbitrary point set, with its exact Euclidean adjoint.

using PartialFSHT: PartialFSHT
using LinearAlgebra: LinearAlgebra
using FINUFFT: FINUFFT

plan = PartialFSHT.Plan(θ, φ; lmax = 2048, tol = 1e-8)
LinearAlgebra.mul!(f, plan, alm)      # synthesis
LinearAlgebra.mul!(alm, plan', f)     # exact adjoint

Evaluate the degree-lmax field on the few rings that meet the data's colatitude interval, apodise, periodise, interpolate with one small NUFFT — Carron & Reinecke's construction (arXiv:2603.17166) with no coefficient rotation, because a per-apply Wigner rotation costs about eight times the ring work it saves.

The public surface is small

mul!(f, plan, alm)          # fast partial-geometry synthesis
mul!(alm, plan', f)         # exact Euclidean adjoint
solve!(alm, plan, f; mode)  # coefficients, with the regularisation named
spectrum(plan, f, w, Wℓ)    # band-powers, coupling handled internally

Everything returns plain arrays of spherical harmonic coefficients or ℓ-indexed band-powers. No Slepian labels, no wrapper types, nothing to carry around alongside the numbers.

Two things worth knowing immediately

Synthesis and the adjoint are exact at any sky coverage. There is no support condition, because synthesis is a function evaluation — the coefficients already define the field everywhere.

Inversion is not. Only ~N_S = (lmax+1)² · f_sky coefficient directions are constrained, and that fraction is f_sky independently of ℓ. So plan' * f is not "the coefficients of my data" — it is the exact adjoint, and therefore the masked field's coefficients. Band-powers via spectrum are the well-posed product; see Recoverability.

PartialFSHT.PartialFSHT — Module
PartialFSHT

Partial-sky spherical harmonic transforms: the operator 𝒴_R : a_{ℓm} ↦ f(Ω_i) on an arbitrary point set, with its exact Euclidean adjoint plan'.

Coefficients are complex orthonormal Condon–Shortley harmonics in FastSphericalHarmonics sph_mode layout. The fast path is a stretched colatitude interval — the field on the few rings that meet the data, apodised, periodised and interpolated with one small NUFFT (Carron & Reinecke, arXiv:2603.17166) — with no coefficient rotation anywhere in the apply. Direct evaluation is the oracle and shares the same block-scaled Legendre recurrence.

Synthesis and the adjoint are exact at any sky coverage. Inversion from a region is not: only ~N_S = (lmax+1)² f_sky directions are constrained, so solve! names its regularisation and recoverability reports what the geometry supports before any work is spent. For "which wavenumbers carry what", spectrum is the well-posed answer.
