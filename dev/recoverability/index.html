Recoverability

Synthesis and the adjoint are exact at any sky coverage. Inversion is not, and the distinction is the one thing a user of a partial-sky transform has to get right.

Three different maps

With $A_{i,(\ell m)} = Y_{\ell m}(\Omega_i)$:

mapwhat it computesexact on a patch?is it "the coefficients"?
synthesis $A$coefficients → values at the pointsyes, alwaysn/a
adjoint $A^\dagger$$\sum_i \overline{Y_{\ell m}(\Omega_i)} f_i$yes, alwaysno
inversionvalues → coefficientsno — rank-deficientthat is the goal, and it is ill-posed

The trap is the middle row. $A^\dagger f$ is exactly computable on a patch but it is not the underlying field's harmonic coefficients — it is the masked field's, mode-coupled and leakage-biased. Computing it and calling the result $a_{\ell m}$ gives a wrong answer. What the adjoint is correct for is being the exact transpose of $A$: what an iterative solver, a likelihood gradient, or a quadratic estimator needs.

Synthesis needs no support condition because it is a function evaluation, not a fit: the coefficients already define the field everywhere, and the DFS extension of a degree-$L$ field is an exact 2-D trigonometric polynomial of degree $(L,L)$, so the NUFFT evaluates a known polynomial from exact Fourier coefficients rather than interpolating scattered data.

The left panel is the bad news and the right panel is the point: individual coefficients are hopeless at small $f_{\rm sky}$, while band-powers at the geometry's own $\Delta\ell$ land within a few percent over most of the range.

What is recoverable

  • The local spectrum, well. With $N_w = \Lambda/\lambda$ wavelengths across the patch, wavenumber magnitude and direction are determined to $\approx 1/N_w$; the resolution is $\Delta\ell \approx \pi/\Lambda$. The correction for the gaps is the direct analogue of the window correction in any 1-D periodogram — a matrix instead of a scalar only because a spherical window couples $\ell$'s.
  • Individual $a_{\ell m}$, no. Only $N_S = (L+1)^2 f_{\rm sky}$ directions are constrained. It is a conditioning failure, not a uniqueness failure: a degree-$L$ field is a polynomial restricted to $S^2$, so exact noiseless patch data determines it — but recovering the $k$-th direction needs data precision $\sim\lambda_k$, and the Slepian eigenvalues fall off a cliff past $N_S$, with a transition band growing only logarithmically in SNR.

Small scales do not help. Within any band the well-determined fraction is $f_{\rm sky}$, independently of $\ell$, because higher $\ell$ brings proportionally more coefficients. 300 wavelengths across a basin make the spectrum excellent without moving that fraction at all.

The barrier is continuous in $f_{\rm sky}$:

geometry$f_{\rm sky}$$N_S/(L+1)^2$$\Delta\ell \approx \pi/\Lambda$

What to do instead

Ordered by how often it is the right answer.

  1. Compare against a global model in coefficient space — window both sides identically and compare in $(\ell,m)$. From the caller's side this is one extra forward transform:

    PartialFSHT.apply_resolution!(ã_model, plan, alm_model, w)   # A† W A
ã_obs = plan' * (w .* f_obs)

    Both sides equal the same operator applied to their own field, so the comparison is exact and unbiased with nothing to deconvolve. This is forward-modelling the model into data space rather than deconvolving data into truth space.

  2. Energy per mode from a region — a band-power spectrum with the mask's own coupling accounted for, via spectrum. Individual $\tilde a_{\ell m}$ are a known but non-invertible mixture; the quadratic aggregate has a tractable bias, $\langle \tilde C_\ell\rangle = \sum_{\ell'} M_{\ell\ell'} C_{\ell'}$, with $M$ built from the mask's own power spectrum. Nothing about the unobserved sky enters. Why this escapes the ill-posedness is counting: coefficients are $(L+1)^2$ unknowns against $\sim N_S$ constraints, the spectrum is $\sim L/\Delta\ell$ unknowns against the same constraints. The cost is variance, not bias: $\Delta C_\ell/C_\ell \approx \sqrt{2/((2\ell+1) f_{\rm sky} \Delta\ell)}$.

  3. Work inside the patch — solve!(…, mode = :minnorm). Optimal there; its field decays outside over the reproducing-kernel scale $\sim 1/L$, so it is the wrong choice if the coefficients then feed a global quantity.

  4. Need a global field — mode = :wiener with a stated prior, i.e. a background and an assimilation step. Minimum-norm is the wrong answer here: a regional feature surrounded by dead calm provokes spurious adjustment.

Two things not to do

Zero-padding. Masking a map to the patch, filling the rest with zeros and running a full-sphere transform creates a discontinuity at the mask edge whose power-law spectral tail aliases into the band. The bandlimited minimum-norm fit has the same "≈zero outside" but no edge and no out-of-band power — the effective taper is the optimally-concentrated one, with mainlobe $\Delta\ell \approx \pi/\Lambda$ and sidelobes at the $(1-\lambda_k)$ level.

Intersecting masks when comparing two regions. Non-overlapping or chiral footprints have a near-empty intersection, and when shape is the signal, intersecting destroys the experiment. What must be common is the spectral resolution: bin both to the coarser $\Delta\ell$, which discards no data and merely declines to claim resolution the narrower region cannot support.

On minimum-norm

It is the least-energy exact fit, not an extreme one, and $AA^\dagger$ is well conditioned at Nyquist patch sampling. The zero-extension is a property of the truncation level, not of minimum-norm: with exact data, uniqueness means it returns the true field and continues correctly outside. Note also that any unique, deterministic estimate must decay outside the data, since the minimum-variance estimate of an unobserved zero-mean quantity is zero — "the pattern continues similarly" is inherently a random draw. Unique and deterministic, or plausible continuation; not both.
